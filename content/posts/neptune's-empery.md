# Backstory

In my hunt for nice poems I stumbled upon Thomas Campion’s *A Hymn in Praise of Neptune* while flipping through *The Oxford Book of English Verse, 1250-1900: Enriched Edition*. I couldn’t help but close my eyes after reading and instead of thinking of grand ocean voyages or swashbuckling buccaneers I thought of those “blue empires” beyond Earth. I attribute part of this chain of thought from reading Ada Limón's beautiful poem [*In Praise of Mystery: A Poem for Europa*](https://poets.org/poem/praise-mystery-poem-europa).

Uranus also came to my mind, its uncanny pale hue and enormity just sitting in the vastness of space. I thought of what I’d read about Enceladus and Europa, icy marbles with oceans tucked away from the eyes of man. And I thought even further than our Solar System, to the news of watery exoplanets[^5] and the tantalizing dream that life beyond what we know it to be could exist in those dark depths. 

Perhaps we will get the chance to see life like that of the Ediacaran or Cambrian Periods on Earth: Sponges and *Kimberella* abounding, the delightful-looking *Anomalocaris*, *Opabinia*, and *Hallucigenia* wandering about[^6], a snapshot of our origins just waiting to be discovered!

{{figure(src="/posts/neptune's-empery/cambrian-smithsonian.jpg", caption="Anomalocaris depicted swimming above, with Hallucigenia (the spiky fellow) center bottom. Credit: Smithsonian Institution", width="600")}}

Or perhaps there is only a sad soup of organic compounds. Just salt, minerals, ice and the of the finitude of our being rendered naked against the cosmos.

Either way, I dream and dream onwards! I let every word of a good poem take me to worlds within and horizons beyond ✨

[^1]: Veteran sailor - [Merriam-Webster](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/sea-dog)

[^2]: Crown - [Merriam-Webster](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/diadem)

[^3]: The Original NASA image caption does not use the term "cryovolcano" but this [BBC Sky at Night Magazine article](https://www.skyatnightmagazine.com/space-science/cryovolcanism-in-the-solar-system) uses the same image with the term "cryovolcanism". I have elected to use that term in this image caption as it feels more closely tied to the words "shrill" and "echoing rock" in the poem (things I would associate with a normal volcano!).

[^4]: Wide dominion - [Merriam-Webster](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/empery)

[^5]: I note here that it seems the majority of news about water on exoplanets usually indicates the water is in a state that is not conducive to the formation/hosting of life but that being said, it isn't a huge stretch of the imagination to imagine a world out there where the water is a bit milder :P. See [this NASA post](https://science.nasa.gov/universe/exoplanets/two-exoplanets-may-be-mostly-water-nasas-hubble-and-spitzer-find/) and [this Space.com post](https://www.space.com/astronomy/exoplanets/astronomers-discover-hot-watery-exoplanet-destined-to-be-swallowed-by-its-star).

[^6]: Kudos to Danna Staaf's *Monarchs of the Sea: The Extraodinary 500-Million-Year History of Cephalopods* for introducing me to the Ediacaran period as well as *Kimberella*, I consulted the book in helping compose this sentence.
