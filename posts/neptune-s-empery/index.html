Neptune's Empery

2026-10-16
#space#poetry#biology 
A Hymn in Praise of Neptune - Thomas Campion

Of Neptune's empire let us sing,
At whose command the waves obey;
To whom the rivers tribute pay,
Down the high mountains sliding:

To whom the scaly nation yields
Homage for the crystal fields
        Wherein they dwell:
And every sea-dog1 pays a gem
Yearly out of his wat'ry cell
To deck great Neptune's diadem2.

The Tritons dancing in a ring
Before his palace gates do make

The water with their echoes quake,
Like the great thunder sounding:

The sea-nymphs chant their accents shrill,
And the sirens, taught to kill
        With their sweet voice,
Make ev'ry echoing rock reply3

Unto their gentle murmuring noise
The praise of Neptune's empery4.

Backstory

In my hunt for nice poems I stumbled upon Thomas Campion’s A Hymn in Praise of Neptune while flipping through The Oxford Book of English Verse, 1250-1900: Enriched Edition. I couldn’t help but close my eyes after reading and instead of thinking of grand ocean voyages or swashbuckling buccaneers I thought of those “blue empires” beyond Earth. I attribute part of this chain of thought from reading Ada Limón's beautiful poem In Praise of Mystery: A Poem for Europa.

Uranus also came to my mind, its uncanny pale hue and enormity just sitting in the vastness of space. I thought of what I’d read about Enceladus and Europa, icy marbles with oceans tucked away from the eyes of man. And I thought even further than our Solar System, to the news of watery exoplanets5 and the tantalizing dream that life beyond what we know it to be could exist in those dark depths.

Perhaps we will get the chance to see life like that of the Ediacaran or Cambrian Periods on Earth: Sponges and Kimberella abounding, the delightful-looking Anomalocaris, Opabinia, and Hallucigenia wandering about6, a snapshot of our origins just waiting to be discovered!

Or perhaps there is only a sad soup of organic compounds. Just salt, minerals, ice and the of the finitude of our being rendered naked against the cosmos.

Either way, I dream and dream onwards! I let every word of a good poem take me to worlds within and horizons beyond ✨
